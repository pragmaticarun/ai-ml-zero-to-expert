Block 1: Concept lesson: clustering for a business question (1h15)

Framing first. "Segment our customers" is not a question. "Find 2–5 groups whose buying patterns differ enough to justify different sales approaches" is. Good segments are: - Distinct: separated in feature space. - Stable: they reappear with other seeds or subsamples. - Interpretable: describable in a sentence. - Actionable.

The dataset: wholesale customers (UCI). 440 clients of a Portuguese wholesale distributor with annual spend on Fresh, Milk, Grocery, Frozen, Detergents_Paper and Delicassen, plus Channel (Horeca = hotel/restaurant/café, or Retail) and Region. We hold out Channel as an external check. It is never used in fitting.

Workflow. 1. Inspect distributions (spend is heavily right-skewed). 2. Transform (log), then scale. 3. Run k-means for a range of k and compare silhouette. 4. Profile each segment with medians. 5. Run stability checks. 6. Compare with the external label. 7. Cross-check with a GMM and BIC. 8. Visualise with PCA (Day 50 covers PCA properly; here it is just a 2-D view).

Block 2: Worked example: reference results (45 min)

code/week-07/day49_week_project.py. Verified: - Skew: Delicassen 11.15, Frozen 5.91, Milk 4.05. The means are far above the medians (Fresh: mean 12,000 vs median 8,504; max 112,151). - Silhouette:

The raw silhouette looks much better (0.57), but it is driven by a few huge customers that sit far from everyone else, and those separate "nicely". After the log transform the structure is weaker but reflects typical customers. A higher silhouette is not automatically a better segmentation. - Profiles (k=2, log-scaled), median annual spend: - Segment 0 (n=252): Fresh 10,348, Grocery 2,406, Detergents 328. Fresh-food-heavy buyers. - Segment 1 (n=188): Grocery 11,527, Milk 7,690, Detergents 4,495. Shelf-goods buyers. - External check against Channel: segment 0 is 244 Horeca and 8 Retail. Segment 1 is 54 Horeca and 134 Retail. ARI is 0.5135. The unsupervised segments rediscovered most of the restaurant/retail split without ever seeing it. - Stability: across 10 single-init runs with different seeds, the minimum pairwise ARI was 0.9909. - GMM BIC: 1: 6596.3, 2: 6434.4, 3: 6269.0, 4: 6312.4, 5: 6410.6. BIC prefers 3 components while silhouette prefers 2 k-means clusters. The methods disagree because they measure different things (likelihood under flexible ellipses vs separation of round groups). Report both, and choose by usefulness. - PCA 2-D view explains 0.4408 + 0.2719 = 71% of the log-scaled variance.

Block 3: Coding lab: the project (2h)

Deliverable: notes/week07_segments.md with figures. 1. Explore (20 min): histograms raw vs log, and a correlation matrix. Which spend categories move together? (Grocery and Detergents_Paper is a known strong pair. Verify it.) 2. Cluster (30 min): k-means for k = 2…7 on log-scaled data. Report silhouette and inertia, and choose k with a written argument. 3. Profile (20 min): a median table per segment and one-line personas. 4. Validate (25 min): a stability ARI over 10 seeds and over 80% subsamples. Then crosstab and ARI against Channel. 5. Alternative (15 min): a GMM with BIC for 1–6 components. Does the 3-component solution split one of your k-means segments? Which one? 6. Recommend (10 min): one paragraph for the sales team.
