Block 1: Concept lesson (1h15)

Intuition. Drop k pins on a map of points. Each point joins its nearest pin. Then move each pin to the centre of its group. Repeat until nothing moves. That is Lloyd's algorithm for k-means. It minimises inertia, the total squared distance from points to their centroid:

J = Σᵢ ‖xᵢ − μ_{c(i)}‖²

Each step (assign, then update) can only lower J, so it converges, but only to a local minimum that depends on the starting pins.

Initialisation. Random starts can put two pins in one blob and none in another. k-means++ picks each new initial centroid with probability ∝ squared distance to the nearest existing centroid, so pins spread out. scikit-learn uses it by default with several restarts (n_init), keeping the lowest inertia.

Choosing k. Inertia always falls as k grows, so you can't minimise it over k. Two heuristics: - Elbow: look for the k where the drop flattens. - Silhouette: for each point, s = (b − a)/max(a, b), where a is its mean distance to its own cluster and b is its mean distance to the nearest other cluster. The average lies in [−1, 1], and higher is better.

Neither is truth. Ask what the clusters are for.

Assumptions. k-means likes round, similar-sized, similar-density clusters, and it needs scaled features (Euclidean distance again).

Block 2: Worked example (45 min)

code/week-07/day47_kmeans.py. Verified: - Blobs (1500 points, 4 true clusters with std 1.0): scratch Lloyd reaches inertia 2681.1 in 9 iterations. scikit-learn reaches 2680.9. ARI against the true labels is 0.8303 (Adjusted Rand Index: 1 is perfect, 0 is chance). - Initialisation matters. Over 20 seeds, random init ranged from inertia 2680.9 to 20954.7. k-means++ ranged from 2680.9 to 3530.5. - Choosing k:

Honest finding: silhouette picks k = 3, but the data was generated with 4 clusters. Two of the true centres are only 2.08 apart (with cluster std 1.0), so they overlap, and silhouette prefers merging them into one well-separated group. The elbow (a big drop to k = 3, a smaller one to 4, then a flat curve) is ambiguous between 3 and 4. This is normal. Validation without labels is a judgement call. - Digits, k = 10 on raw pixels: 43 iterations in about 35 ms. ARI against the true digit is 0.6512, and purity is 0.7891. The majority-digit map [2, 8, 4, 3, 1, 5, 1, 6, 0, 7] shows that digit 1 owns two clusters and digit 9 owns none. 9s merge with similar-looking digits.

Going deeper (optional)

k-means is a hard-assignment, equal-spherical-variance limit of the Gaussian mixture fitted by EM (tomorrow). Shrink the GMM's shared variance to 0 and its soft responsibilities become hard nearest-centroid assignments. That is the bridge to probabilistic clustering.

Common mistakes / how to debug

  • Using ARI to choose k when you have no labels. You don't, in real life.
  • Clustering one-hot columns with Euclidean k-means. Consider k-modes or Gower distance.
