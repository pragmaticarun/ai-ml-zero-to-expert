Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

Write the BPTT recursion for ∂L/∂h_{t−1} in a tanh RNN.

∂L/∂h_{t−1} = W_hᵀ (diag(1 − h_t²) ∂L/∂h_t) + direct loss terms at t−1.

Q2.

With W_h spectral radius 0.51, what was |∂L/∂h| at t = 0 (T = 50)?

1.01e-19.

Q3.

What happened on the Day-78 memory task at T = 20?

MSE 2.303, worse than predicting 0 (0.931): training failed.

Q4.

Give the LSTM cell update equation.

c_t = f ⊙ c_{t−1} + i ⊙ g; h_t = o ⊙ tanh(c_t).

Q5.

Parameter counts of RNN, GRU, LSTM with I = 10, H = 32.

1,408; 4,224; 5,632.

Q6.

What did forget-bias 1 change on Day 79, and what happened at T = 50?

At T = 20 the LSTM went from 0.7520 to 1.0; at T = 50 all models stayed near chance (about 0.25).

Q7.

What is the SGNS loss for one positive pair and k negatives?

−log σ(u_ctx·v) − Σ_k log σ(−u_k·v).

Q8.

Why were raw cosines misleading in the first Day-80 version?

Anisotropy: all vectors shared a direction, so cos(king, the) was 0.874; centring fixed it.

Q9.

Exact-match at length 16 without vs with attention?

0.000 vs 0.292.

Q10.

Why does fp16 need loss scaling? Use the Day-82 example.

1e-8 underflows to 0 in fp16; scaling by 65,536 keeps it (6.55e-4) and it unscales to 9.997e-9.

Q11.

Name the three JAX transformations and what the 101 ms measured.

grad, jit, vmap; 101 ms was the first-call compile time of a fresh jit.

Q12.

Validation bits/char of the trigram and of the GRU LM after 1,500 steps.

2.984 and 2.159.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: written in full (Phase 3). Titles match SYLLABUS.md.

Week goals

  • Derive backpropagation through time and measure why plain RNN gradients vanish or explode.
  • Use LSTMs and GRUs, count their parameters, and know their limits on long-range memory.
  • Train word embeddings (skip-gram with negative sampling) and read them critically.
  • Build an encoder-decoder and see attention fix its bottleneck.
  • Understand number formats and mixed precision; profile a training run; write a small Rust/PyO3 data kernel.
  • Get a quick tour of JAX (grad, jit, vmap).
  • Train a character-level language model and compare it with count baselines (weekly project).
flowchart LR

  B --> C[Day 80 embeddings]

Compute note. CPU-only box. Models are small and training is short. The point is to see mechanisms, not to set records.

Datasets: Tiny Shakespeare (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/karpathy/char-rnn/master/data/tinyshakespeare/input.txt, 1,115,394 characters, public-domain text), cached in data/ by code/common.py. Synthetic sequence tasks generated by the scripts. MNIST and CIFAR-10-small as in earlier weeks.
