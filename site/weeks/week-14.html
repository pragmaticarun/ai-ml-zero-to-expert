Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

Give the shape of the attention score tensor for x of shape (B, T, D) with h heads.

(B, h, T, T).

Q2.

What does a padding mask block, and why?

Keys at padding positions, so real tokens' outputs do not depend on how much padding is in the batch.

Q3.

What happens to a softmax row that is fully masked?

It becomes NaN (0/0).

Q4.

Why is attention without positions permutation-equivariant, and why is that a problem?

Weights depend only on content, so reordering the inputs only reorders the outputs; word order would be invisible.

Q5.

State the key property of RoPE.

Rotating q and k by position-dependent angles makes q·k depend only on the relative offset (verified: −0.36481 at offsets of 3 from several starts).

Q6.

Count the parameters of a pre-LN block of width d without attention biases.

4d² + 8d² + 5d + 4d = 12d² + 9d (197,760 at d = 128).

Q7.

What did the no-warmup stability test show about post-LN?

At lr 1e-2 post-LN stalled at loss 3.326 while pre-LN reached 2.355; at 3e-3 both reached about 2.38.

Q8.

Convert a loss of 1.625 nats/token on characters to bpc.

1.625 / ln 2 = 2.344 bpc.

Q9.

Why does BPE encoding apply merges by rank?

To reproduce the training merge order, so the same text gives the tokens the model was trained on.

Q10.

Why did the rayon encoder lose to serial Rust in one run?

Per-word work is tiny and the cores were busy, so thread scheduling cost more than parallelism saved (42.6 ms vs 7.3 ms).

Q11.

State the Chinchilla rule of thumb and one reason to train past it.

About 20 training tokens per parameter for compute-optimal loss (Hoffmann et al., 2022); train longer to get a smaller model that is cheaper to serve.

Q12.

Why must models with different tokenizers be compared in bits per character?

Per-token loss depends on how much text a token covers; bpc normalises by the text itself.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: written in full (Phase 4). Titles match SYLLABUS.md. Every number below was printed by a script in code/week-14/ on this box (Python 3.13.5, torch 2.15.0+cpu, 8 CPU threads, no GPU). Wall-clock times were measured while other jobs shared the CPU. Treat them as rough and re-time on your own machine.

Week goals

  • Implement multi-head self-attention with causal and padding masks and match PyTorch to float precision.
  • Understand why attention needs positional information; implement sinusoidal encodings and RoPE and verify RoPE's relative-position property.
  • Assemble a pre-LN transformer block, count its parameters exactly, and compare pre-LN with post-LN.
  • Write and train a tiny GPT on CPU and beat a bigram baseline.
  • Write byte-level BPE from scratch, compare it with a pretrained tokenizer, and speed up the encoder in Rust.
  • Run a miniature scaling experiment and read the published scaling-law papers critically.
  • Train the GPT on BPE tokens (weekly project).

Compute note (CPU vs GPU). Everything here trains on a CPU in minutes, with models under a million parameters. On a single modern GPU the same code (with .cuda()) would run roughly one to two orders of magnitude faster per step, which would let you train ~10-100× larger models in the same time. The ideas do not change with scale; the sample quality does.

Datasets: Tiny Shakespeare (1,115,394 characters, cached by code/common.py; 90/10 train/val split by position). Day 96 also downloads the SmolLM2-135M tokenizer from the Hugging Face Hub (HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M, Apache-2.0, download verified on this box) into data/cache/hf.

Shared code: code/week-14/_gpt.py (the GPT used through Day 119) and code/week-14/_bpe.py (the tokenizer). Rust: code/week-14/rust_bpe (maturin develop --release).
