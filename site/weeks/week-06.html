Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

How do k and feature scaling affect k-NN?

Small k means high variance and large k high bias. Without scaling, the widest-range feature dominates (wine: 0.69 unscaled vs 0.95 scaled).

Q2.

Our d=1000 nearest/farthest ratio was about what, and what does it imply?

0.874. Distances concentrate, so "nearest" carries little information.

Q3.

State the naive Bayes assumption, and why computing in log space matters.

Features are independent given the class. Logs avoid underflow when multiplying many small probabilities.

Q4.

Did NB beat logistic regression at small m in our run?

No. Logistic regression led at every size; NB only plateaued earlier.

Q5.

Write the soft-margin SVM objective.

½‖w‖² + C Σ max(0, 1 − yᵢ(w·xᵢ + b)).

Q6.

What does a large RBF γ do?

It shrinks each point's influence radius, so boundaries get wiggly (overfitting, or collapse with small C).

Q7.

Compute the Gini impurity for [6 pos, 4 neg].

0.48.

Q8.

What happened to test accuracy when the breast-cancer tree grew past depth 3?

It fell from 0.9161 to 0.9021 as the tree became pure (overfitting).

Q9.

Write the variance of an average of B trees with correlation ρ.

ρσ² + (1 − ρ)σ²/B.

Q10.

What fraction of rows is out-of-bag, and how close was OOB to test on adult?

About 36.8% (1/e). OOB 0.8550 vs test 0.8582.

Q11.

In gradient boosting with squared loss, what does each tree fit?

The residuals y − F, i.e. the negative gradient of the loss.

Q12.

Which model won the Week 6 project, with what test AUC?

HistGB with native categoricals: test ROC-AUC 0.9281.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: written in full. Titles match SYLLABUS.md.

Week goals

  1. Build k-NN, Gaussian naive Bayes, a hinge-loss linear SVM, a CART decision tree and gradient boosting from scratch, and match each against scikit-learn.
  2. Understand each model's inductive bias, meaning what kind of pattern it finds easily, so you can pick one for a problem before trying it.
  3. Know the ensemble ideas behind most winning tabular models: bagging decorrelates, and boosting corrects residuals.
  4. Ship a leakage-proof tabular pipeline on the adult income data.
flowchart LR

  NB[Naive Bayes: generative] --> S
  T[Decision tree] --> RF[Random forest: bagging]
  T --> GB[Gradient boosting: residuals]
  RF --> P[Project: adult income pipeline]
  GB --> P

Datasets this week (all fetched by code, nothing is committed): scikit-learn's bundled digits, wine and breast-cancer data, synthetic moons, California housing, and adult (US 1994 census income, 48,842 rows, 14 features, fetched from OpenML via common.load_adult()).
