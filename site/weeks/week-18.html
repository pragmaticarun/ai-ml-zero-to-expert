Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

(Day 120) In the capstone, retrieval recall@5 was 0.79 but answer accuracy was 0.25. Where did the failure analysis place most errors?

Generation: 8 of 12 were generation errors, 1 was a retrieval miss, and 3 were correct.

Q2.

(Day 120) Why did the simulated int8 model show no speed-up?

The weights were rounded but stored in fp32 again, so the arithmetic was the same. It showed quality loss only.

Q3.

(Day 121) Define the return G_t, and give its lower bound when every reward is −1 and γ < 1.

G_t = Σ_k γ^k r_{t+k+1}; the lower bound is −1/(1−γ).

Q4.

(Day 121) Write V^π as a linear solve.

V = (I − γP_π)^{-1} r_π.

Q5.

(Day 122) What does policy iteration alternate, and why does it terminate?

It alternates policy evaluation and greedy improvement. Each step improves the policy, and a finite MDP has finitely many deterministic policies.

Q6.

(Day 122) Why did VI take 571 sweeps at γ 0.99 but 145 at 0.9 on 4x4 FrozenLake?

The error shrinks by about γ per sweep, so the number of sweeps needed grows as γ approaches 1.

Q7.

(Day 122) Why can V(start) = 0.006 sit next to a success rate of 0.596?

V is discounted (γ 0.9 makes a long, safe path worth almost nothing), while success counts any arrival within gymnasium's 100-step limit.

Q8.

(Day 123) Write the SARSA and Q-learning targets. Which one learned the cliff-edge path?

SARSA: r + γQ(s', a'). Q-learning: r + γ max_a Q(s', a). Q-learning learned the edge path (−13 in all 10 seeds).

Q9.

(Day 123) Why did a constant-α MC plateau?

Each update moves V a fixed fraction towards a noisy return, so the error stops falling at a noise floor (MC α 0.03 stayed at 0.099).

Q10.

(Day 124) What is "semi" about semi-gradient TD, and what did it cost on the 1000-state walk?

It ignores the target's dependence on w. It converged to a biased fixed point: RMS error 0.131, against 0.061 for MC and a best possible of 0.053.

Q11.

(Day 125) Name DQN's two stabilisers, and say what happened without the target network.

Experience replay and a target network. Without the target network, all 3 seeds collapsed to about 9-step episodes.

Q12.

(Day 126) Give the three metrics used in the project, and one case where they disagreed.

max Q(start) vs V*, policy agreement, and greedy success in gymnasium. Example: Q-learning seed 0 at 20k episodes had max Q 0.407 (close to 0.415) but only 0.376 success.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: Day 120 (the Month-4 capstone and checkpoint exam) is written in full and closes Month 4. Days 121-126 (Month 5 opens: RL fundamentals) are written in full; their numbers come from code/week-18/day121_*.py to day126_*.py (CPU only, gymnasium 1.4.0, each under about 2 minutes), with logs in code/week-18/out/. Day 120's numbers come from code/week-18/day120_capstone.py, run once on this box (CPU only, 8 threads, torch 2.15.0+cpu, transformers 5.21.1, about 50 seconds), with its log in code/week-18/out/day120_capstone.log.

Compute note. The capstone runs on CPU because every model is small: the MiniLM embedder and cross-encoder, and SmolLM2-135M-Instruct. The int8 quantisation is simulated: weights are rounded and then stored in fp32 again, so it shows quality loss but no speed-up. On a GPU, or with a real int8/int4 kernel (for example llama.cpp, which this box cannot build because it has no g++ or cmake), you would also measure memory and speed gains, and you could use a larger generator. That matters most here, because the generator is where this system fails.

Models and data. These are the same verified, locally cached models as Week 17: sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2, cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2 and HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M-Instruct. The corpus is weeks/week-01.md to week-16.md. Nothing new was downloaded.
