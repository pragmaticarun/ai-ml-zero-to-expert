Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

Why does random search beat grid search on the same budget? Use the Day-85 numbers.

With only 3 × 3 values, a grid tries just 3 learning rates; random search tried 9. Grid best 0.9382, random best 0.9434 (at lr 2.58e-3, a value the grid could not try).

Q2.

What was the Day-85 seed-noise standard deviation, and which comparison did it make meaningful?

0.0007 over 5 seeds; it showed that the 0.0052 gap between random and grid winners (about 7 std) is probably real.

Q3.

Give the two baselines and the capstone's validation accuracy on Day 86.

Majority class 0.1016, logistic regression 0.3688, small ResNet 0.7656.

Q4.

What happened at epoch 8 of the capstone run, and why was it not a reason to stop?

Val acc fell from 0.6152 to 0.3728 while train loss kept falling; it was a one-epoch wobble near the one-cycle peak, and it recovered to 0.7656.

Q5.

Which single ablation cost the most, and by how much?

Removing batch norm: −0.3508 (val acc 0.3536).

Q6.

Which two ablations scored slightly higher than the full model, and how should the report describe them?

No augmentation (+0.0068) and no residual connections (+0.0064): "no measurable effect at an 8-epoch budget with one seed".

Q7.

What did the half-data ablation suggest about where to invest next?

Half data cost 0.0864, twice the half-width cost (0.0432): more data, or augmentation as its proxy, should help most.

Q8.

Give the test accuracy, with and without flip-TTA.

0.7736 without and 0.7816 with flip-TTA.

Q9.

Name the top confusion pair and the share of errors explained by the top five pairs.

cat → dog (74 errors); the top five explain 39% of the 566 errors.

Q10.

Define ECE and give its Day-88 value.

Bin predictions by confidence and take the size-weighted mean of |accuracy − confidence|; 0.0203 with 10 bins.

Q11.

At an abstention threshold of 0.9, what coverage and accuracy did the model get?

Coverage 41.5%, accuracy 0.9730.

Q12.

Why is attention divided by √d_k? Give one measured value.

The variance of q · k grows with d_k, which saturates the softmax; e.g. the std was 31.25 at d_k = 1024 and 0.98 after scaling.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: written in full (Phase 3). Titles match SYLLABUS.md. Every number below was printed by a script in code/week-13/ on this box (Python 3.13.5, torch 2.15.0+cpu, 8 CPU threads, no GPU). Wall-clock times were measured while other jobs shared the CPU, so treat them as rough. Accuracies are deterministic for a fixed seed.

Week goals

  • Run a hyperparameter search you can defend: a fixed budget, a log file, a validation set, and a measured seed-noise floor.
  • Build a Month-3 capstone image classifier (a small ResNet on CIFAR-10-small) and beat two baselines by a clear margin.
  • Run ablations that change one thing at a time, and report the surprising rows honestly.
  • Do a full error analysis: per-class accuracy, confusion pairs, input slices, calibration, abstention, and a hand-tagged error sheet.
  • Review Months 1-3 with runnable drills, then deliver a written capstone report and sit the checkpoint exam.
  • Start Month 4 by writing scaled dot-product attention from scratch (weekly project).

Compute note. CPU-only. The capstone trains in about 3 minutes for 15 epochs on 15,000 images. The ablations take about 30 minutes in total; run them while you read.

Datasets: MNIST (Day 85, via code/week-10/_mnist.py) and CIFAR-10-small from OpenML dataset 40926 (Days 86-90, see data/README.md for why OpenML is used). The split is fixed with seed 0: 15,000 train, 2,500 validation, 2,500 test. The test set is used once, on Day 88, after every choice is frozen.
