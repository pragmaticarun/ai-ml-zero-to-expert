Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

Compute (2, −1, 3)·(1, 4, 2).

2 − 4 + 6 = 4.

Q2.

Cosine similarity of (1, 0) and (1, 1)?

1/√2 ≈ 0.707.

Q3.

Shape of (5 × 3)(3 × 7)ᵀ? (Careful.)

Trick question: (5 × 3)(3 × 7) is 5 × 7, and its transpose is 7 × 5.

Q4.

FLOPs to multiply two 256 × 256 matrices?

2·256³ ≈ 33.6M FLOPs.

Q5.

Rank the measured speeds: Python loops, naive Rust and BLAS (orders of magnitude from your run).

Python ≈ 50-70 MFLOP/s < naive Rust ≈ 8.8 GFLOP/s < BLAS ≈ 230-330 GFLOP/s (on this box; yours will differ).

Q6.

Write the normal equation, and say why lstsq is preferred.

AᵀA x = Aᵀb. lstsq and QR avoid squaring the condition number and handle rank deficiency.

Q7.

What does a condition number of 1e12 imply in float64?

You may lose about 12 of the ~16 significant digits.

Q8.

Eigenvalues of [[2, 1], [1, 2]]?

1 and 3.

Q9.

What fraction of variance did the top 10 PCA components of digits explain?

73.8%.

Q10.

Name three properties of an orthogonal projection matrix P (or of its residual).

Symmetric, idempotent (P² = P), and the residual (I − P)y is orthogonal to the column space.

Q11.

Storage for a rank-k SVD of an h × w image?

k(h + w + 1).

Q12.

Why must PCA be fit only on the training set?

Fitting on test data leaks its statistics into the model (leakage).

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Month 1, Foundations. Time budget: 42 h. Companion reading (optional): Mathematics for Machine Learning, ch. 2-4 (https://mml-book.github.io/), and the 3Blue1Brown linear algebra series (https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/linear-algebra).

Week goals

  1. Treat a data point as a vector and a dataset as a matrix. Read every model as "matrix in, matrix out".
  2. Compute and interpret dot products, norms, cosine similarity and projections.
  3. Do matrix multiplication by hand, in pure Python, in Rust and with BLAS, and explain the roughly 1000× gaps.
  4. Solve linear systems properly (solve, not invert), understand conditioning, and fit a line by least squares.
  5. Explain eigenvectors and the SVD visually, implement PCA from scratch, and match scikit-learn.
