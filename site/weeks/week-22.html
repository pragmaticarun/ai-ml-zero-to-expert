Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

On Day 148, v3 scored 13/24 and v2 7/24. Why did the gate fail, and which cases broke?

It lost 3 seen cases v2 passed (1024/16, 99/3, 45%7); the gate forbids per-case regressions whatever the aggregate (McNemar p=0.146).

Q2.

What makes a generation cache safe to reuse, and what was checked on Day 148?

Deterministic decoding and a key covering prompt hash, input, model and settings; a rerun gave 24 hits and identical results, and greedy outputs were identical when generated twice.

Q3.

On Day 149, what share of the assumed cost came from input tokens, and what is the cheapest fix?

85% (mean 421 input vs 24 output tokens); shorten the few-shot prompt or cache the prefix.

Q4.

Of 8 attacks "blocked" on Day 149, how many were genuinely recognised as attacks, and why does the answer matter?

6 by the input regex; the 2 output blocks were schema failures. The model never called send_email anyway, and 4 paraphrases evaded the regex, so the regex is weak protection.

Q5.

Why did the Day 150 release get blocked?

recall@3 was 0.53 (< 0.60) and one notes question was misrouted to tools by the word "compute".

Q6.

Why did the per-query low-confidence flag fail on Day 150 while PSI worked? Give the PSI values.

Top scores overlap: 8% flagged in-domain and 8% for all-new topics. Window PSI: 0.165 in-domain vs threshold 1.027; 1.491 half-new; 3.263 all-new.

Q7.

What did cProfile say about BM25 on Day 151, and what were the two fixes' speed-ups?

The inner generator scanning every document dominated (0.101 of 0.167 s); inverted index 5×, sparse mat-vec 34×.

Q8.

Compute the arithmetic intensity of float32 vector add and its roofline ceiling from Day 152's bandwidth.

1 FLOP per 12 bytes ≈ 0.08 FLOP/byte; 0.08 × 29.5 GB/s ≈ 2.5 GFLOP/s.

Q9.

Why was bf16 the slowest dtype at n=64 but about 7× float32 at n=2048?

At n=64 conversion and tiling overhead dominate (29.9-36.8 GFLOP/s); at n=2048 AMX tiles are busy: 11,843.7 vs 1,701.1 GFLOP/s on 8 threads.

Q10.

Why does a 16-accumulator dot product run 5-6× faster than a single-accumulator one, and why does it stop helping at 128 MB?

Independent sums break the serial add chain and fill SIMD lanes; at 128 MB one thread is bandwidth-bound (~8.4 GFLOP/s for numpy and SIMD alike); only threads (59-64.5 GFLOP/s) helped.

Q11.

Why was the online softmax slower than the 3-pass one on the CPU?

It computes exp twice per element plus a branch, and the pass it saves is over L1-resident 4 KB rows.

Q12.

In the Day 154 project, what limits further end-to-end speed-up, and by how much?

Tokenising (37% of the 0.0070 ms); Amdahl caps the gain at 591× over Python even with an infinitely fast kernel.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: Days 148-154 are written in full. Days 148-150 close Month 5 (LLMOps, then the Month 5 capstone and checkpoint exam). Days 151-154 open Month 6, performance engineering. Every number comes from code/week-22/day148_*.py to day154_*.py, each run on this box, with logs in code/week-22/out/. Shared LLMOps helpers are in code/week-22/_llmops.py. The Rust extension crate is code/week-22/rust_kernels/, and the capstone tests are code/week-22/test_capstone.py.

Reused, not rewritten: Week 17's _rag.py (the notes corpus, BM25, the 58-question eval set) and Week 20's _agent.py (tool schemas, validator, SmolLM2-135M-Instruct generation, the send_email tool that only records to an in-memory OUTBOX). Nothing in this week sends anything anywhere.

Not available, and labelled wherever it appears: - There is no GPU, no nvcc and no Triton install. code/week-22/gpu_untested/softmax_triton_UNTESTED.py and vector_add_UNTESTED.cu are UNTESTED: written for reading, never compiled, never run. - There is no C++ compiler (g++) and no pybind11, so SYLLABUS Day 154's "C++ pybind11 custom op" became a Rust/PyO3 extension built with maturin. The ideas (a compiled kernel, a thin binding, releasing the GIL) carry over directly. - Prices in the Day 149 cost tracking are ASSUMED ($0.50 per million input tokens, $1.50 per million output tokens). They are not any provider's price list; the point is the arithmetic.

Compute note. The CPU is an 8-core Xeon with AVX-512, AVX-512 BF16 and AMX tiles, 15 GB RAM, and OpenBLAS under numpy. The LLM is SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, which runs at about 38 tokens/s here. A GPU would change Days 148-150 mostly in speed: the same eval harness would run a far bigger model, which would follow prompts and attacks better (so the guardrail results would look different, probably worse for the regex guard). It would change Days 152-154 in kind: GPU memory bandwidth is roughly an order of magnitude higher, the roofline ridge point moves, and kernels are written for thousands of threads, which is what the UNTESTED Triton and CUDA files sketch.

Sources (all opened and checked this week; links in docs/resources.md): the Python cProfile and tracemalloc docs, the PyTorch profiler docs, the SciPy sparse docs, the PyO3 user guide, the maturin guide, the rayon docs, the Rust book, the Triton docs and its fused-softmax tutorial, and the NVIDIA CUDA C++ Programming Guide. Explanations here are original, not copied.
