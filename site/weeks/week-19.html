Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

(Day 127) Write the REINFORCE gradient and say why environment dynamics do not appear in it.

∇J = E[Σ_t ∇log π(a_t|s_t)·G_t]; the trajectory probability factorises into policy terms and dynamics terms, and the dynamics do not depend on θ.

Q2.

(Day 127) Why does a state-dependent baseline add no bias? What did a constant baseline do to gradient spread at init?

E_a[∇log π·b(s)] = b(s)·∇Σ_a π = 0. Spread fell from 0.436 to 0.324.

Q3.

(Day 127) Why did the baseline runs use more environment steps, and why does that matter?

They learned longer episodes under a fixed episode budget, so they saw 45,666-48,298 steps against 8,383-19,571; the comparison is confounded (fixed on Day 133).

Q4.

(Day 128) Write the one-step TD advantage. Why bootstrap on truncation?

δ = r + γV(s') − V(s). Truncation at 500 steps is not a real terminal state, so its value is not 0.

Q5.

(Day 128) What happened in A2C n = 1, seed 2?

It collapsed to greedy eval 9.3 at 20k and 30k steps (2,117 episodes), then recovered to 500.

Q6.

(Day 129) Write PPO's clipped objective and explain the A < 0 case.

E[min(rA, clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)A)]. With A < 0, there is no further gain from pushing r below 1−ε.

Q7.

(Day 129) What did removing clipping do to approximate KL and to evaluation?

Max KL rose from 0.017-0.019 to 2.352-2.790 and the clip fraction from about 0.06 to about 0.5; evaluations repeatedly collapsed to about 10 steps, and seed 1 ended at 121.

Q8.

(Day 130) Rank Thompson, UCB1 and ε-greedy by regret in the log, and explain the surprise.

Thompson 30.5 < ε-greedy 112.7 < UCB1 183.3. UCB1's bonus over-explores at a 2,000-pull horizon.

Q9.

(Day 130) Why did LinUCB beat ε-greedy so clearly in the recommender?

Different users prefer different items; only LinUCB uses the context (CTR 0.876 vs 0.399).

Q10.

(Day 131) Why did naive shaping break even value iteration, and what is potential-based shaping?

The shaped MDP's optimum is to loop for repeated +0.1 rewards. Potential-based: F = γΦ(s') − Φ(s), which leaves the optimal policy unchanged (but was not reliably faster: 9/10 seeds, median 38 vs 54).

Q11.

(Day 132) Where does the KL go in InstructGPT-style PPO and in GRPO, and why did our GRPO not reach π*?

PPO: in the per-token reward, −β·log(π/π_ref). GRPO: added to the loss with the k3 estimator. Std normalisation changes the reward scale and identical groups get zero advantage, so the effective β differs: GRPO reached reward about 0.93-0.95 at KL about 2.4-2.5, against π*'s 0.799 at 1.193.

Q12.

(Day 133) Summarise the project result and one limitation.

At about 50k steps on fresh seeds, PPO reached 500 greedy on 5/5, A2C 4/5 (mean 499.8), REINFORCE 3/5 (mean 421.3, min 137.5). Limitation: five seeds, one easy-to-saturate environment, final policy only.

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Status: Days 127-133 (Month 5, policy-gradient RL, exploration, reward shaping and the RL-for-LLMs link-back) are written in full. Every number comes from code/week-19/day127_*.py to day133_*.py, each run once on this box (CPU only, torch.set_num_threads(1), torch 2.15 CPU, gymnasium 1.4.0), with logs in code/week-19/out/. Shared helpers are in code/week-19/_pg.py.

Compute note. Everything here uses 4-64-64-2 MLPs on CartPole-v1, tabular grids, or bandits with 5-20 arms. A GPU would not speed these up: the cost is the Python loop that steps the environment one transition at a time, not matrix maths. What a GPU (or several) changes is the Day 132 setting: PPO/GRPO on a real language model, where every "action" is a token from a 50k-way softmax, a rollout is autoregressive generation, and you hold a policy, a reference model and (for PPO) a value model in memory at once. None of that is run here; Day 132 uses a 5-answer toy so that the exact optimum is known.

Sources used for Day 132 (all opened and checked): PPO, https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347; InstructGPT, https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155; DPO, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290; DeepSeekMath (introduces GRPO), https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300.
