Interactive weekly quiz (12 questions)

Q1.

Derivative of J(w) = (4w − 8)² at w = 1?

2(4w − 8)·4 = 2(−4)(4) = −32.

Q2.

Why use central differences, and what h works well in float64?

The error is O(h²) instead of O(h). h ≈ 1e-5 to 1e-6.

Q3.

Write the vectorised gradient of J = (1/2m)‖Xw − y‖².

∇J = Xᵀ(Xw − y)/m.

Q4.

Give the gradient-check relative-error thresholds.

< 1e-7 good, ~1e-5 suspicious, > 1e-3 a bug.

Q5.

Backprop: J = (wx + b − y)², with x = 1, y = 3, w = 1, b = 0. What are dJ/dw and dJ/db?

e = 1·1 + 0 − 3 = −2, so dJ/dw = 2e·x = −4 and dJ/db = 2e = −4.

Q6.

Why do gradients accumulate when a variable is used twice?

The total derivative sums the contributions over all paths (the multivariable chain rule).

Q7.

f = x² + 10y². What is the largest stable α, and why?

α < 2/λ_max = 2/20 = 0.1. Otherwise the y-direction factor |1 − 20α| exceeds 1.

Q8.

P(sick | +) for prevalence 1%, sensitivity 95% and FPR 5%?

≈ 0.161.

Q9.

Var of the mean of n i.i.d. samples with variance σ²?

σ²/n.

Q10.

95/100 correct. Roughly what is the std of the accuracy estimate?

√(0.95·0.05/100) ≈ 0.022 (2.2%).

Q11.

MLE of a coin's p from k heads in n flips?

k/n.

Q12.

Under what noise assumption is least squares the MLE? What loss do Bernoulli labels give?

Gaussian noise. Bernoulli labels give log-loss (binary cross-entropy).

Week overview, goals, project and quiz text

Month 1, Foundations. Time budget: 42 h. Optional reading: Mathematics for Machine Learning, ch. 5 (vector calculus) and ch. 6 (probability) (https://mml-book.github.io/).

Week goals

  1. Read a derivative as "the slope: how much the output changes per unit nudge of the input", and compute it analytically and numerically.
  2. Compute gradients of multivariable functions, including the vectorised MSE gradient Xᵀ(Xw − y)/m, and gradient-check them.
  3. Apply the chain rule on a computational graph. Build a ~40-line reverse-mode autograd and train a line with it.
  4. Run gradient descent and diagnose learning rates (too small, good, divergent) from the curvature.
  5. Use probability, Bayes' rule, distributions, expectation and variance, and the CLT, checking each by simulation.
  6. Derive maximum-likelihood estimates and show that least squares = MLE under Gaussian noise.

Weekly project (Day 21): MLE three ways (closed form, grid, gradient ascent), with a write-up that connects MLE to the cost functions of Week 4.

flowchart LR
  P[parameters w] --> M[model ŷ = f x;w]
  M --> L[loss J]
  L -- dJ/dw via chain rule --> G[gradient]
  G -- w ← w − α·grad --> P
