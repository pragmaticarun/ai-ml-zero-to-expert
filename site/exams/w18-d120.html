Day 120: Month 4 checkpoint exam (15 questions; 90 minutes; closed notes, then mark yourself)

Day 120 · 15 questions parsed from weeks/week-18.md · Day 120 page

Q1.

Write scaled dot-product attention and explain the √d_k factor.

softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V. Without the scaling, the dot products of unit-variance vectors have standard deviation √d_k, which saturates the softmax.

Q2.

What does RoPE do to queries and keys, and why does it give relative-position behaviour?

It rotates each pair of query and key dimensions by an angle proportional to the token's position. The dot product of rotated q and k then depends only on the position difference.

Q3.

Draw a pre-norm transformer block (attention and MLP sub-layers with residuals).

x → x + Attn(Norm(x)) → x + MLP(Norm(x)); a final norm comes before the output head.

Q4.

How does BPE build its vocabulary?

Start from characters or bytes, then repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair into a new token until the vocabulary reaches its target size.

Q5.

What is perplexity, and how does it relate to cross-entropy?

Perplexity = exp(mean cross-entropy per token in nats). It is the "effective number of choices" per token.

Q6.

What does LoRA train, and how many parameters does a rank-r adapter on a d×k matrix add?

It trains low-rank matrices B (d×r) and A (r×k) with W' = W + (α/r)·BA, while W stays frozen. That adds r(d + k) parameters.

Q7.

Why are only assistant tokens included in the SFT loss with a chat template?

The model should learn to produce answers, not to predict the user's prompt. Masking the user and system tokens focuses the gradient on the responses.

Q8.

Write the Bradley-Terry preference probability, and say what a reward model learns from it.

P(a ≻ b) = σ(r(a) − r(b)). The reward model learns a scalar score whose differences predict human (or labelled) preferences.

Q9.

Write the DPO loss. What role does β play?

−log σ(β[(log π(y_w) − log π_ref(y_w)) − (log π(y_l) − log π_ref(y_l))]). β sets how strongly the policy may move away from the reference (it acts like a KL strength).

Q10.

Name three pitfalls in LLM evaluation from Week 16.

Any three of: - test-set contamination; - tiny eval sets with wide confidence intervals; - LLM-judge biases (position, length, self-preference); - prompt-format sensitivity; - string-match brittleness.

Q11.

Why does hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) often beat either one alone?

They make different errors: BM25 matches rare exact terms, while dense retrieval matches paraphrases. Rank fusion combines both without calibrating scores (Week 17: recall@5 0.67 for BM25 and 0.64 for dense alone, 0.76 fused).

Q12.

Why does int4 per-tensor quantisation fail while group-wise int4 works?

Outlier weights set a single huge scale, so most weights round to zero (Day 116: perplexity 1,561,367.93). Group-wise scales follow the local range (group-64: 36.89, against 26.29 for fp32).

Q13.

What problem do GPTQ and AWQ solve that plain RTN does not, and how does each approach it?

RTN ignores the inputs, but output error depends on the activations. GPTQ compensates the rounding error in the remaining weights using the Hessian XᵀX. AWQ scales up the weight channels that meet large activations. On Day 117, int4 per-channel perplexity fell from 64.84 (RTN) to about 44 with both methods.

Q14.

Why does a KV cache speed up decoding, and what is its memory cost per token?

Past keys and values do not change, so each decode step processes only the new token (Day 118: 27.3 → 67.8 tok/s). Memory per token is 2 × layers × KV heads × head dim × bytes; for SmolLM2 that is 23.6 MB per sequence at 512 tokens in fp32.

Q15.

In the capstone, retrieval delivered the answer in 11 of 12 prompts, but accuracy was 3 of 12. What do you conclude, and what are the main limits of that conclusion?

The generator, not retrieval, limits quality (8 of 9 errors were generation errors). Limits: - only 12 questions, written by the notes' author; - string-match scoring; - one tiny model; - simulated quantisation; - CPU only.
