Day 150: Month-5 checkpoint exam (15 questions; 60 minutes; covers Days 121-150)

Day 150 · 15 questions parsed from weeks/week-22.md · Day 150 page

Q1.

Write the Bellman expectation equation for V^π and say what changes in the optimality equation.

V^π(s) = Σ_a π(a|s) Σ_{s'} P(s'|s,a)[r + γV^π(s')]. The optimality equation replaces the average over π with a max over actions.

Q2.

Why does value iteration converge for γ < 1?

The Bellman optimality operator is a γ-contraction in the max norm, so repeated application converges to its unique fixed point.

Q3.

Q-learning vs SARSA: which is off-policy, and why does SARSA learn a safer path on a cliff?

Q-learning is off-policy (its target uses max over next actions); SARSA uses the action actually taken, including exploratory ones, so it values cliff-edge states lower under ε-greedy and learns the safer path.

Q4.

What two tricks stabilise DQN, and what failure does each address?

A replay buffer (breaks correlation between consecutive samples) and a target network (stops the target moving with every update).

Q5.

In REINFORCE, why does subtracting a baseline not bias the gradient?

The expected score-function gradient of a baseline that does not depend on the action is zero, since Σ_a ∇π(a|s) = 0.

Q6.

What does PPO's clipped objective prevent?

Policy updates that move the probability ratio far from 1 in one step, which destabilises training.

Q7.

Give one example of reward hacking and one defence.

E.g. an agent looping to collect a shaping bonus instead of finishing; defence: potential-based shaping, or reward only the true goal and audit behaviour.

Q8.

In RLHF with PPO, what is the KL penalty against, and why is it there? How does DPO avoid a reward model?

Against the reference (SFT) policy, to stop the policy drifting into reward-model exploits and losing fluency. DPO optimises a closed-form loss on preference pairs that implicitly defines the reward via the policy/reference log-ratio.

Q9.

UCB vs Thompson sampling: how does each balance exploration?

UCB adds an optimism bonus that shrinks with visit count; Thompson samples from the posterior and acts greedily on the sample.

Q10.

Why validate an agent's tool call against a JSON schema before executing it?

Model outputs are untrusted; validation catches malformed or out-of-range arguments before any side effect.

Q11.

Define pass^k for agents and why it is stricter than pass@k.

pass^k: the task succeeds in all k independent trials; it measures reliability, while pass@k (any of k) measures capability.

Q12.

What is indirect prompt injection, and which defence does not depend on detecting it?

Instructions hidden in retrieved or tool content that the model follows. An allow-list plus human approval for side-effect actions works without detection.

Q13.

Name three things that can cause training/serving skew.

Different preprocessing code, feature staleness or leakage, different library versions or dtypes (any three).

Q14.

What does PSI measure, and why choose its alert threshold from resampled reference windows?

Shift between binned distributions; resampling in-domain windows gives the natural PSI spread for that window size, so the threshold has a known false-alarm rate (Day 150: 99th percentile, 1.027).

Q15.

A prompt change raises the pass rate but fails the regression gate. What do you do?

Read the per-case diff; decide whether the regressions matter; fix the prompt or explicitly accept and document; grow the eval set; never just ship on the aggregate.
